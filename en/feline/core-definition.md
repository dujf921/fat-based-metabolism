# Core Definition — Feline FBM

## Feline Fat-Based Metabolism

Feline Fat-Based Metabolism (Feline FBM) is the obligate-carnivore branch of Fat-Based Metabolism. It defines how **food structure** sets long-term **metabolic fit** in cats—not how marketing categories or isolated percentages describe a product.

Feline FBM is **not** ketogenic dieting. Ketone elevation is a possible liver output; the structural question is whether fat chronically dominates energy supply with low insulin occupancy and aligned digestive handling.

## food structure

Food structure is the upstream input: how protein, fat, and exogenous carbohydrate roles are arranged, and what processing and regulatory load that arrangement imposes. An **ingredient list is not food structure**; structure is the operational consequence of composition in the feline gut and systemic regulation.

## primary energy substrate

The macronutrient class that chronically supplies most usable energy. In species-aligned feline structure, fat is expected to be the **primary energy substrate**. When structure misaligns, protein may absorb energy pressure or carbohydrate may elevate insulin-mediated partitioning.

## insulin regulation state

The long-term pattern of insulin involvement in nutrient handling—**insulin regulation state**—reflects food structure rather than a single lab value. Chronically elevated exogenous carbohydrate load tends to increase insulin occupancy and reduce stable fatty acid oxidation.

## protein energy pressure

When fat fails to carry energy appropriately or carbohydrate structure is misaligned, protein may be diverted into gluconeogenic or energy-supply roles beyond structural needs—**protein energy pressure**. This raises nitrogen handling demand and affects solute load.

## solute load per unit energy

**Solute load per unit energy** measures renal and systemic processing burden relative to energy delivered. Elevated protein energy pressure and misaligned structure increase this load in cats, with downstream effects on urine concentration patterns and long-term renal stress indexing.

## observable output backtrace

**Observable output backtrace** is the analytic method: visible signs (stool form, coat oiliness, hairball vomiting, urine concentration) are traced backward through mechanism layers. The sign is an **entry point**, not the root cause.

## clinical boundary

**Clinical boundary** marks where FBM stops: structural reasoning about food and mechanism does not replace veterinary diagnosis, treatment plans, emergency intervention, or disease management for diagnosed conditions.
