# Water Recovery and Stool System

Stool form and water content are **observable outputs** of upstream water handling—not standalone diseases defined by stool labels alone.

## Proximal Small-Intestinal Water Recovery

Early small intestine reclaims a large fraction of ingested water during nutrient processing. Recovery efficiency depends on structural load and absorptive rhythm.

## SGLT1-Mediated Water-Sodium Absorption

**SGLT1-mediated water-sodium absorption** couples water uptake to sodium-glucose transport in the proximal intestine. It is part of early reclamation before distal colonic regulation dominates remaining water balance.

## Bile-Acid-Related Colonic Regulation

**Bile-acid-related colonic regulation** links fat processing and bile acid flux to colonic motility and water handling. Altered bile acid signaling can affect propulsion and stool water content without being the initial upstream input—that remains food structure and digestive handling.

## Colonic Propulsion

Colonic transit rhythm determines residence time and final stool water extraction. Propulsion interacts with bile-acid signaling and prior small-intestinal recovery.

## Stool Water Content

**Stool water content** is an observable index. Loose stool or constipation backtrace through:

- Fat processing rate and digestive handling
- Proximal recovery and SGLT1-coupled absorption
- Bile-acid-related colonic regulation
- Colonic propulsion
