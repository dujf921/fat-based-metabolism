## Feline Fat-Based Metabolism

Species-specific FBM for cats—obligate carnivores whose expected food structure is animal-protein and fat dominant with minimal exogenous carbohydrate load.

## food structure

Integrated macronutrient roles and processing demands in feline diet—not equivalent to an ingredient list or guaranteed analysis table read in isolation.

## primary energy substrate

In aligned feline structure, fat is expected to chronically dominate usable energy supply; misalignment forces protein into compensatory energy roles.

## exogenous carbohydrate load

Dietary carbohydrate entering insulin-mediated pathways; structurally elevated load conflicts with obligate carnivore substrate expectations.

## insulin regulation state

Long-term insulin partitioning posture shaped by food structure—not a single glucose reading.

## fatty acid oxidation pathway

Primary route for fat-derived energy when substrate dominance and insulin state permit stable oxidation.

## protein energy pressure

Pressure when protein is used beyond structural/amino needs because fat or carbohydrate structure fails to carry energy appropriately.

## solute load per unit energy

Renal processing burden per unit energy—sensitive to protein-as-energy misalignment in cats.

## fat processing rate

Effective throughput of gastric release, emulsification, enzymatic processing, absorption, and lymphatic transport.

## bile salt dispersion

Emulsification of dietary fat in the small intestine; quality affects absorption and distal bile-acid signaling.

## bile-acid-related colonic regulation

Colonic water and motility responses linked to bile acid flux after fat processing.

## SGLT1-mediated water-sodium absorption

Early proximal recovery of water with sodium-glucose cotransport.

## sebaceous synthesis and release

Glandular sebum production and export—modeled as processing rate, not dermal fat leakage.

## sebum processing rate

Balance of sebaceous output versus clearance at the skin surface.

## secondary microbial layer

Microbial overgrowth following altered sebum or local environment—downstream, not primary cause.

## observable output backtrace

Tracing coat, stool, vomit, and urine signs backward through mechanism layers to food structure.

## clinical boundary layer

Limit where structural reasoning ends and clinical diagnosis or emergency care begins.

## gastric emptying speed

Rate at which gastric contents enter duodenal processing—relevant to hair residence and nutrient handling rhythm.

## hair entanglement probability

Likelihood of ingested hair forming a mass influenced by emptying speed, motility, and gastric residence time.

## metabolic fit

Alignment between food structure and feline obligate carnivore metabolic expectations over time.
