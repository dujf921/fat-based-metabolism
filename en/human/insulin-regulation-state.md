# Insulin Regulation State

Insulin regulation state describes the degree to which insulin occupies long-term energy allocation.

In Human FBM, insulin is not treated only as a blood-glucose marker. It is treated as an allocation signal that changes whether stored fat can be mobilized and whether fatty-acid direct energy can dominate.

The causal chain is:

external carbohydrate input → glucose scheduling → insulin occupancy → substrate allocation → fatty-acid availability

A lower insulin regulation state allows fatty-acid direct energy to remain structurally available when total energy is sufficient and lean mass is preserved.

This is not a treatment claim. It is a structural description of energy allocation under different input conditions.
