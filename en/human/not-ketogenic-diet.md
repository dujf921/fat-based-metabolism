# Human FBM Is Not Ketogenic Diet

Human FBM is not defined by ketogenic-diet identity.

Ketogenic diets are commonly described through carbohydrate restriction and ketone production. Human FBM uses a different control point: long-term energy substrate structure under low insulin occupancy and sufficient energy availability.

Ketone production may appear under some conditions, especially low carbohydrate input or fasting. It is not the defining goal of Human FBM.

The defining structure is:

external carbohydrate reduction → insulin occupancy reduction → fatty-acid direct energy → stable energy sufficiency → lean-mass preservation

Human FBM therefore rejects the shortcut:

high ketones = better metabolism

The framework evaluates whether the system can maintain stable energy availability, body-fat mobilization, lean-mass stability, and long-term operating state without relying on exogenous carbohydrate scheduling.
