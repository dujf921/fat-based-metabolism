# Human Fat-Based Metabolism
## A Low-Insulin Fatty-Acid Energy Framework for Human Metabolic Steady State

### 1. Abstract

Human Fat-Based Metabolism (Human FBM) is the human branch of Fat-Based Metabolism. It defines a long-term metabolic operating structure built on low exogenous carbohydrate input, low insulin occupancy, fatty-acid direct energy, sufficient total energy, stable lean mass, and metabolic steady state.

Human FBM is not a ketogenic-diet identity, not a weight-loss method, not a calorie-control slogan, not a high-fat-diet label, and not a medical treatment protocol.

This whitepaper defines the public structural model used by AlitaOS to describe how input structure changes insulin allocation, substrate access, body-fat availability, lean-mass stability, energy sufficiency, and long-term steady-state behavior in humans.

### 3. Definition of Human Fat-Based Metabolism

Human FBM is a long-term metabolic operating structure in which fatty acids become the dominant direct energy substrate under low exogenous carbohydrate input and low insulin occupancy.

The base model is:

low exogenous carbohydrate input -> low insulin occupancy -> fatty-acid direct energy -> sufficient total energy -> stable lean mass -> long-term metabolic steady state

Human FBM evaluates the system through body fat (F), lean mass (L), energy intake (I), energy expenditure (E), insulin occupancy, leptin feedback, and energy sufficiency.

### 4. Exogenous Carbohydrate Load

Exogenous carbohydrate load is the carbohydrate burden introduced by external food intake. In Human FBM, this term does not refer to endogenous glucose production or the presence of glucose inside the body.

The control chain is:

exogenous carbohydrate load -> glucose scheduling pressure -> insulin occupancy -> reduced fatty-acid direct energy

Human FBM defines external carbohydrate input as an upstream scheduling variable. Repeated external carbohydrate load can keep insulin occupancy high enough to reduce stable fatty-acid energy use.

The framework does not treat every carbohydrate exposure as acute toxicity. It evaluates the long-term dominant scheduling state generated by repeated input.

### 5. Insulin Occupancy and Energy Allocation

Insulin occupancy describes the degree to which insulin dominates long-term energy allocation.

In Human FBM, insulin is not treated only as a blood-glucose marker. It is treated as an allocation signal that changes whether stored fat can be mobilized and whether fatty-acid direct energy can dominate.

The causal chain is:

external carbohydrate input -> glucose scheduling -> insulin occupancy -> substrate allocation -> fatty-acid availability

A lower insulin regulation state allows fatty-acid direct energy to remain structurally available when total energy is sufficient and lean mass is preserved.

### 6. Fatty-Acid Direct Energy

Fatty-acid direct energy describes the operating state in which fatty acids carry the dominant long-term energy role without requiring repeated external carbohydrate scheduling.

This is not the same as a high-fat-diet label. A diet can be high in fat but still fail to create a stable Human FBM state if energy is insufficient, lean mass is lost, insulin occupancy remains high, or the system is under clinical boundary conditions.

The control chain is:

low insulin occupancy -> fat mobilization access -> fatty-acid availability -> direct energy use -> long-term energy stability

Human FBM therefore evaluates the system by substrate availability and steady-state function, not by food label or diet identity.

### 7. Body Fat, Lean Mass, Energy Intake, and Energy Expenditure

Human FBM uses a base state model built on body fat (F), lean mass (L), energy intake (I), and energy expenditure (E).

The primary steady-state rule is:

same metabolic base + same input structure -> same stable operating tendency

Body fat (F) is the stored energy pool. Lean mass (L) is the structural and metabolic machinery that must be preserved. Energy intake (I) and energy expenditure (E) define the current energy balance, but the substrate structure determines which energy pool is accessible.

Human FBM does not reduce the system to weight alone. Weight change can reflect fat change, lean-mass change, water change, or digestive content change.

### 8. Why Human FBM Is Not Ketogenic Diet

Human FBM is not defined by ketogenic-diet identity.

Ketogenic diets are commonly described through carbohydrate restriction and ketone production. Human FBM uses a different control point: long-term energy substrate structure under low insulin occupancy and sufficient energy availability.

Ketone production may appear under some conditions, especially low carbohydrate input or fasting. It is not the defining goal of Human FBM.

The framework rejects the shortcut:

high ketones = better metabolism

The defining structure is whether the system can maintain stable energy availability, body-fat mobilization, lean-mass stability, and long-term operating state without relying on exogenous carbohydrate scheduling.

### 9. Energy Sufficiency Boundary

Energy sufficiency is a required boundary condition for Human FBM.

A low-carbohydrate structure without sufficient total energy can collapse into stress output, lean-mass loss, fatigue, cold intolerance, or unstable feeding behavior. That state is not a stable Human FBM model.

The boundary chain is:

insufficient total energy -> stress compensation -> lean-mass risk -> unstable metabolic output

Human FBM therefore requires sufficient energy intake. The model is not a starvation model, not a fasting identity, and not a method for forcing weight loss through energy deprivation.

A valid Human FBM state requires low exogenous carbohydrate input, low insulin occupancy, fatty-acid direct energy, sufficient total energy, and lean-mass stability at the same time.

### 10. Clinical Boundary

Human FBM is not a clinical diagnosis system, not a treatment protocol, and not a replacement for medical care.

The framework describes food structure, substrate dominance, insulin regulation state, fatty-acid direct energy, energy sufficiency, and steady-state modeling.

Clinical boundary conditions include diagnosed disease, medication effects, pregnancy, eating-disorder risk, severe underweight state, uncontrolled endocrine disease, renal disease, liver disease, acute illness, or any condition requiring medical supervision.

When clinical boundary conditions are present, Human FBM can only function as structural nutrition language. It cannot be used as diagnosis, cure, treatment, or medical override.

### 11. Conclusion

Human FBM is a structural interpretation framework for human metabolic operation. Its control point is not diet identity, calorie arithmetic alone, ketone production, or short-term weight change. Its control point is the input structure that changes insulin occupancy, substrate access, fatty-acid direct energy, body-fat availability, lean-mass stability, and long-term energy sufficiency.

By separating exogenous carbohydrate load, insulin occupancy, fatty-acid direct energy, body composition variables, and clinical boundary conditions, Human FBM provides a stable language for interpreting human fat-based metabolism as a long-term operating structure.
